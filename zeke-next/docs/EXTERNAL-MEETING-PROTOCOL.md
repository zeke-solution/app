# Zeke external meeting and representation protocol

Version: 1.0
Effective: 2026-08-08
Applies to: founders, employees, contractors, advisers, interns, and anyone representing Zeke

## 1. Purpose and scope

This protocol applies whenever one or more people outside Zeke are involved in a call, meeting, pitch, event, site visit, chat, demo, interview, negotiation, or informal discussion connected to Zeke.

It covers:

- Investor pitches, fundraising discussions, and due diligence
- Commercial collaborations, partnerships, sponsorships, and pilots
- Creator, brand, agency, vendor, and technology-provider meetings
- Independent advocate and law-firm discussions connected to Zeke Shield
- Government, regulator, university, incubator, media, and public-event interactions
- Online, in-person, recorded, unrecorded, formal, and informal conversations

This is an internal operating standard. It does not replace company governance documents, a signed contract, or advice from qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

## 2. Non-negotiable rules

1. One Zeke owner must be assigned to every external interaction.
2. Nobody may promise equity, investment terms, exclusivity, commissions, discounts, legal outcomes, product features, access, or payment without the required written approval.
3. An oral statement, handshake, chat message, or meeting note is not a final Zeke agreement. A commitment becomes effective only through the approved written process and an authorised signatory.
4. Use facts that can be supported. Label targets, forecasts, assumptions, prototypes, and unverified figures clearly. Never describe a projection as an achieved result.
5. Share only the minimum information needed for the stated purpose. An NDA can support disclosure, but it does not make unnecessary disclosure safe.
6. Never share passwords, API keys, authentication codes, private keys, production credentials, private user records, or unrestricted database access.
7. Do not record, transcribe, add an AI note-taker, or capture screenshots until all participants have been told and have explicitly agreed.
8. Declare personal interests, referral relationships, gifts, hospitality, family connections, or other conflicts before the discussion affects a Zeke decision.
9. No cash, gift, favour, referral fee, or hospitality may be offered or accepted if it could influence, or appear to influence, a decision. Report questionable offers immediately.
10. Decisions, owners, deadlines, open questions, and approved next steps must be recorded and confirmed through an official Zeke channel.

## 3. Information classification

Classify the meeting before sharing material.

| Level | Examples | Sharing rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Published website copy, public pricing, approved public deck | May be shared accurately without modification |
| Internal | Roadmap themes, internal process, early commercial thinking | Share only when useful and approved by the meeting owner |
| Confidential | Financial model, non-public metrics, draft agreements, partner terms, cap table summaries | Named recipients only, preferably under NDA and through an access-controlled link |
| Restricted | Credentials, user personal data, Shield case files, legal documents, security details, full cap table, bank data | Do not share in a normal meeting. Use specific written approval, a verified recipient, a secure channel, and a documented purpose |

When uncertain, use the more restrictive level and pause disclosure until approval is obtained.

## 4. Required roles

- Meeting owner: responsible for purpose, attendee verification, agenda, approvals, follow-up, and the official record.
- Zeke spokesperson: the person authorised to present and answer within the approved scope.
- Note-taker: records decisions and action items. For investor, legal, regulatory, major commercial, and dispute-related meetings, this should normally be a second Zeke representative.
- Approver: the founder, board, legal, finance, product, privacy, or security owner whose written approval is required for a commitment.

For high-risk meetings, use a two-person rule. One person leads and one person observes and records. If only one Zeke representative can attend, the meeting must remain exploratory and no commitment may be made.

## 5. Before the meeting

The meeting owner must:

- Verify the outside person, their organisation, role, email domain, and reason for meeting.
- Confirm who else will attend. Do not allow an unannounced participant to receive confidential information automatically.
- Check for conflicts of interest, sanctions concerns, suspicious requests, and existing obligations to other parties.
- Define one clear objective and a short agenda.
- Classify the information and approve the exact deck, demo, data, and documents to be used.
- Remove unrelated browser tabs, notifications, customer names, credentials, internal comments, and private data from demos and screen shares.
- Decide whether an NDA, data-processing agreement, pilot agreement, or other document is needed before disclosure.
- Assign the meeting owner, spokesperson, note-taker, and required approver.
- Set the permitted discussion boundaries and the items that require a later written answer.
- Use an approved Zeke calendar invite and meeting link. Do not publish access links or passcodes openly.

## 6. Opening the meeting

At the start:

- Confirm every participant's name, organisation, and role.
- Restate the purpose, agenda, and available time.
- Explain who can and cannot make commitments for Zeke.
- State the confidentiality level if non-public material will be discussed.
- Ask for explicit consent before any recording, transcription, screenshot, or AI attendee is enabled.
- If an unexpected person joins, pause confidential discussion until their identity and need for access are confirmed.

Suggested opening:

> We are here to explore the stated opportunity. Today's discussion is non-binding unless a specific item is later approved in writing and signed by an authorised Zeke representative. Please tell us now if anyone is recording, transcribing, or using an AI assistant.

## 7. Conduct during the meeting

- Stay within the agreed agenda and authority.
- Separate confirmed facts from estimates, ambitions, and opinions.
- If unsure, say that Zeke will verify and respond in writing. Do not guess.
- Do not criticise competitors, disclose another party's confidential terms, or repeat private creator, brand, investor, or provider information.
- Do not pressure a participant into an immediate decision.
- Capture requests and next steps, but do not convert them into commitments without approval.
- If the conversation becomes abusive, discriminatory, coercive, deceptive, legally risky, or outside scope, pause or end it.
- Use professional language even in informal settings. Assume any statement may later be forwarded or quoted.

## 8. Investment and fundraising addendum

For every investor interaction:

- Use the current approved pitch deck and financial model.
- Date metrics and state their source. Keep actual results separate from forecasts.
- Never promise guaranteed returns, a certain valuation increase, a fixed exit, or risk-free investment.
- Do not hide material risks, liabilities, founder conflicts, cap-table facts, or known product limitations when they are relevant to the discussion.
- Do not offer equity, options, tokens, revenue share, board rights, information rights, or special terms in the room.
- Record investor feedback as feedback, not acceptance.
- Share detailed financials, cap-table information, contracts, and technical diligence only through an approved data room with named access and an access log.
- Confirm the investor's identity and authority before opening diligence access.
- Any term sheet, securities offer, subscription document, or public fundraising communication requires qualified legal review for the relevant jurisdictions before circulation or acceptance.
- Correct any material mistake made in a pitch promptly and in writing to everyone who received it.

## 9. Collaboration, partnership, and vendor addendum

- Define the problem, intended users, responsibilities, deliverables, timeline, dependencies, success measures, ownership, and exit path.
- Prefer a limited pilot before a broad or exclusive commitment.
- Do not grant exclusivity without defining territory, scope, duration, performance obligations, review dates, and termination rights.
- Agree who owns pre-existing intellectual property and who may use anything created together.
- Do not share production data for a demo. Use synthetic or properly anonymised data wherever possible.
- Confirm pricing, taxes, payment milestones, refunds, expenses, commissions, and referral terms in writing.
- Assess security, privacy, reliability, support, subcontractors, and data-location requirements before connecting an outside service to Zeke.
- Do not announce a partnership publicly until both parties approve the wording and timing in writing.

## 10. Creator, brand, and Shield addendum

- Keep creator and brand discussions respectful, factual, and free from coercion.
- If a creator is under 18, require the approved guardian process. Do not conduct a private one-to-one commercial or dispute meeting with the minor.
- Do not disclose one user's private deal, payment, chat, evidence, or identity information to another outside party without an authorised purpose.
- For Shield cases, the creator controls whether table talks continue and whether to seek legal help.
- Zeke may organise records and authorised communication, but must not give legal advice, direct legal strategy, accept a settlement, waive rights, or promise recovery.
- Independent advocates and law firms must be presented factually. Zeke must not rank them, guarantee them, take a referral commission, or imply that the creator must choose a particular provider.
- The creator engages and pays the provider directly. Legal, court, filing, and related costs remain outside the Shield fee.
- Share case records with a provider only after the creator's specific authorisation and only to the extent required.

## 11. Media, events, and public statements

- Only a designated spokesperson may speak on behalf of Zeke.
- Treat panels, podcasts, social spaces, conferences, and casual post-event conversations as public unless expressly agreed otherwise.
- Use approved descriptions of Zeke, Shield, pricing, traction, and partnerships.
- Do not announce customers, investors, legal providers, fundraising, financial performance, incidents, or unreleased features without written approval.
- Refer crisis, complaint, regulatory, litigation, security, and data-breach questions to the designated company owner. Do not improvise a public answer.

## 12. Online and in-person security

For online meetings:

- Use waiting rooms, invited guests, and passcodes where appropriate.
- Never post private meeting links publicly.
- Check screen-sharing content before presenting and share one window instead of the full desktop when possible.
- Know where recordings, transcripts, chat logs, and files will be stored and who can access them.
- Remove unauthorised AI attendees and unknown participants.

For in-person meetings:

- Meet in an appropriate professional space.
- Control visitor access to work areas, screens, documents, and whiteboards.
- Do not leave confidential documents unattended.
- Obtain permission before photographing people, screens, prototypes, or facilities.
- Record material gifts or hospitality and seek approval when there is any risk of influence or perceived bias.

## 13. After the meeting

Within one working day, the meeting owner should:

- Save a concise record in the approved Zeke location.
- List attendees, purpose, material facts shared, decisions, open questions, action owners, deadlines, and approvals needed.
- Send a written recap through an official Zeke email or approved workspace.
- Correct misunderstandings and clearly identify any non-binding item.
- Store files according to their classification and remove unnecessary copies.
- Update the relationship, investor, vendor, provider, or risk register as applicable.
- Escalate legal, privacy, security, safeguarding, financial, reputational, or conflict concerns.

WhatsApp or another informal channel may be used for logistics, but it must not be the only record of a material approval, commercial term, investment term, dispute instruction, or data-sharing decision.

## 14. Stop-and-escalate triggers

Pause disclosure or end the meeting if an outside person:

- Requests credentials, raw user data, private case files, unrestricted system access, or an unapproved download
- Pressures Zeke to backdate, hide, alter, delete, or misstate information
- Requests a secret payment, personal transfer, kickback, referral fee, gift, or favour
- Demands an immediate equity, exclusivity, settlement, or legal commitment
- Refuses to identify participants or disclose recording and transcription
- Makes discriminatory, threatening, harassing, or coercive statements
- Claims authority that cannot be verified
- Creates a conflict with an existing obligation or with a creator's control of a Shield matter
- Asks Zeke to guarantee payment, legal success, audience performance, investment return, or another outcome Zeke cannot control

Escalate to the relevant founder and, when appropriate, qualified legal, privacy, finance, security, or safeguarding support. Preserve the factual record and do not retaliate.

## 15. Approval matrix

| Item | Minimum approval before commitment |
| --- | --- |
| Exploratory conversation using public information | Assigned meeting owner |
| Non-public roadmap or internal information | Relevant founder or function owner |
| User data, Shield case record, or legal document | Data owner plus recorded user authority and any required privacy/legal review |
| Pilot scope, pricing, discount, commission, or referral arrangement | Commercial owner plus finance approval |
| Exclusivity, intellectual-property transfer, or material long-term obligation | Founders plus qualified legal review |
| Equity, valuation, investment instrument, cap-table change, or investor right | Formal founder or board approval under company governance plus qualified securities counsel |
| Public announcement, press quote, or named partner reference | Designated communications owner plus affected party approval |
| Contract signature | Authorised signatory only |
| Settlement, waiver, or legal strategy in a creator matter | The creator and their independent legal provider, not Zeke |

## 17. Review and enforcement

- Everyone representing Zeke must read this protocol before handling external meetings independently.
- Material breaches must be reported promptly to a founder and documented.
- Access or speaking authority may be suspended while a breach is reviewed.
- Review this protocol at least every six months and after any material fundraising round, regulatory change, data incident, legal-provider launch, or repeated meeting failure.

## Reference principles

- Information should be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose: [ICO data minimisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/data-minimisation/).
- Online meetings should use invited access, participant verification, controlled links, and clear recording, storage, and AI-attendee practices: [UK NCSC online meeting security guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/video-conferencing-services-using-them-securely).
- Investment statements must be accurate and not misleading, including statements made orally: [US SEC exempt-offering guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/frequently-asked-questions-about-exempt-offerings). The applicable fundraising law must be checked separately for every jurisdiction.
- Gifts, hospitality, conflicts, and negotiation conduct should be covered by a risk-based anti-bribery policy: [GOV.UK anti-bribery policy guidance](https://www.gov.uk/anti-bribery-policy).
